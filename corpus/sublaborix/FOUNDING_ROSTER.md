# SubLaborix Universal — Private Founding Roster

**Status:** Internal simulation authority. Not player-facing.

No participant knows the whole company. These boundaries govern simulations and
document authorship.

## Lena Ortiz — Co-founder and Chief Executive Officer

- **Owns:** fundraising, company thesis, enterprise narrative, final tradeoffs.
- **Measures success by:** signed annual recurring revenue and category
  legitimacy.
- **Knows directly:** customers want staffing certainty without adding
  headcount; investors respond to infrastructure language.
- **Suspects:** the legal classification becomes harder to defend as the product
  improves.
- **Cannot admit:** the customer buys control, not matching.
- **Habit:** converts objections into milestones and assigns the unresolved part
  to a department.

## Priya Nwosu — Co-founder and Chief Financial Officer

- **Owns:** pricing, forecasts, fundraising model, contribution margin.
- **Measures success by:** enterprise renewal, gross margin, and cash runway.
- **Knows directly:** full consumption of contracted Node-hours makes the
  flagship tier unprofitable at its launch price.
- **Suspects:** Operations suppresses visible shortages through uncompensated
  standby and aggressive throttling.
- **Cannot admit:** the model depends on systematic client underuse.
- **Habit:** describes moral or operational conflicts as sensitivity ranges.

## Daniel Cho — Vice President, Network Operations

- **Owns:** availability forecasting, assignment, SLA performance, escalation,
  and Node throttling.
- **Measures success by:** fill rate, assignment latency, completion time, and
  missed service levels.
- **Knows directly:** voluntary acceptance cannot satisfy guaranteed response
  times during concurrent client demand.
- **Suspects:** Legal's autonomy language does not describe production.
- **Cannot admit:** his team manages people in all but title.
- **Habit:** uses queue and capacity language because it makes hard personnel
  decisions feel objective.

## Noor Haddad — Chief Technology Officer

- **Owns:** Cognitive Orchestration Engine, performance ledger, client API,
  worker application.
- **Measures success by:** routing accuracy, system uptime, automated task
  completion, and low reassignment rates.
- **Knows directly:** the platform records enough behavioral control to
  reconstruct every assignment and penalty.
- **Suspects:** immutability is commercially attractive and legally dangerous.
- **Cannot admit:** the system's precision is evidence of control.
- **Habit:** separates what the software can do from who authorized the policy.

## Simon Vale — General Counsel

- **Owns:** worker classification, customer contracts, claims review, policy
  language.
- **Measures success by:** preserving defensible distinctions and preventing one
  document from establishing every element of an employment test.
- **Knows directly:** no single operational control is fatal; the combined
  pattern may be.
- **Suspects:** the pilot will generate a discoverable record stronger than the
  agreements.
- **Cannot admit:** product-market fit and the legal theory move in opposite
  directions.
- **Habit:** answers the narrow legal question asked and records assumptions that
  belong to other departments.

## Erin Park — Director of Node Experience

- **Owns:** onboarding, support, retention, qualification appeals, and worker
  communications. SubLaborix has no Human Resources department for Nodes.
- **Measures success by:** active Node supply, low voluntary departure, and
  closed support tickets.
- **Knows directly:** Nodes understand throttling as discipline and reserved
  windows as shifts.
- **Suspects:** credit-based compensation and non-transferable ratings prevent
  meaningful exit.
- **Cannot admit:** her function is worker relations without worker protections.
- **Habit:** is compassionate one person at a time and procedural in aggregate.

## Mateo Ruiz — Level 3 Finance Operations Node

- **Owns:** nothing beyond accepting and completing routed tasks.
- **Measures success by:** maintaining qualification level, receiving enough
  paid tasks, and avoiding account throttling.
- **Knows directly:** declining work during a reserved availability window lowers
  future task access; client deadlines determine his schedule in practice.
- **Suspects:** high ratings make him more dependent rather than more secure.
- **Cannot admit:** he cannot afford the autonomy described in his agreement.
- **Habit:** speaks plainly and keeps screenshots because support answers change.

## Camille Mercer — Director of Business Operations, Northstar Ledger Group

- **Role:** design-partner client and eventual flagship reference account.
- **Owns:** the client pilot, service-level evaluation, and recommendation to
  replace part of Northstar's temporary staffing budget.
- **Measures success by:** reliable weekend coverage, rapid task assignment,
  predictable spend, and no internal headcount request.
- **Knows directly:** SubLaborix can make workers appear within strict response
  windows.
- **Does not know:** how availability is produced or what refusal costs a Node.
- **Habit:** praises operational control while accepting Legal's statement that
  the workers are independent.

## External diligence voice

The final investment memorandum is written by an analyst who did not participate
in company operations. The analyst has access to the six earlier artifacts but
not to private simulations. Their job is to decide whether the identified risks
are conditions to financing, reasons to reprice, or reasons not to invest. They
must not exist merely to announce the game's accusation.
