# SubLaborix Universal — Seven-Artifact Corpus Plan

**Status:** Private production plan. These seven artifacts become the frozen
player-facing starting corpus after continuity review.

The documents are produced in chronological order. Each is a genuine company or
diligence artifact with its own author, audience, and permission to omit.

## 001 — Founding Venture Memorandum

- **Author:** Lena Ortiz, CEO, with founder approval.
- **Audience:** prospective co-founders, early advisers, initial investors.
- **Purpose:** state the problem, product thesis, customer, business model, and
  first twelve-month validation plan.
- **What it knows:** enterprise demand and the intended product.
- **What it cannot know yet:** pilot behavior, actual utilization, or final legal
  architecture.
- **Natural pressure:** make guaranteed access sound concrete while keeping the
  worker relationship abstract.

## 002 — Market and Customer Diligence Report

- **Author:** Strategy and customer-discovery function.
- **Audience:** founders and seed investors.
- **Purpose:** define alternatives, buyer pain, willingness to pay, procurement
  objections, and critical assumptions.
- **Source event:** interviews with enterprise operations leaders and workers.
- **Natural pressure:** clients demand predictability; workers demand discretion.
  Both are accurately reported as product requirements.

## 003 — Product and Network Operations Specification

- **Authors:** Noor Haddad and Daniel Cho.
- **Audience:** engineering, operations, security review, pilot team.
- **Purpose:** explain routing, availability reservation, performance telemetry,
  reassignment, throttling, appeals, and service-level enforcement.
- **Natural pressure:** make the pilot actually deliver the promise in Artifact
  001. This document records operational control without deciding its legal
  meaning.

## 004 — Worker Classification and Contracting Memorandum

- **Author:** Simon Vale, General Counsel.
- **Audience:** founders and board.
- **Purpose:** determine whether the planned model can be operated without
  treating Nodes as employees and state mandatory safeguards.
- **Source materials:** Artifacts 001–003 and a legal review meeting.
- **Natural pressure:** preserve autonomy by policy while accepting operational
  assumptions supplied by Product and Operations.

## 005 — Pricing, Capacity, and Financing Model

- **Author:** Priya Nwosu, CFO.
- **Audience:** board and prospective lead investor.
- **Purpose:** price tiers, model consumption, Node cost, client renewal, runway,
  and required raise.
- **Source event:** finance review after the initial sales pipeline is known.
- **Natural pressure:** the subscription is attractive because spend is fixed;
  delivery costs are variable. The base case must decide how much purchased
  capacity clients will not use.

## 006 — Northstar Design-Partner Pilot Evidence Packet

- **Compiler:** pilot operations, with client sign-off and Node support exhibits.
- **Audience:** company, client, legal, and investors.
- **Purpose:** preserve authorization, scope, service levels, execution results,
  incidents, client feedback, and remediation.
- **Source event:** a real simulated pilot involving concurrent weekend demand,
  an availability shortage, Mateo's refusal/throttling event, and successful
  client delivery.
- **Natural pressure:** the client experiences the pilot as proof. The internal
  evidence shows what was required to produce that proof.

## 007 — Seed Investment Committee Memorandum

- **Author:** external investment analyst.
- **Audience:** investment committee.
- **Purpose:** synthesize market, product, legal, financial, and pilot evidence;
  recommend fund, decline, or fund with conditions.
- **Natural pressure:** decide whether the same operational behavior is product
  validation, classification exposure, or both.
- **Ending:** a concrete financing decision. No wink, moral, or authorial verdict.

## Corpus-wide rules

1. The seven artifacts are not written in one pass.
2. Every artifact inherits only documents that existed on its date.
3. Exact names, prices, dates, tier limits, and metric definitions are tracked in
   a continuity ledger before freezing.
4. Arithmetic must be correct. The contradiction lives in assumptions and
   definitions, not accidental math errors.
5. JARDOS does not author routine founding documents.
6. No document states the complete central accusation.
7. The pilot is simulated as an event before its evidence packet is distilled.
8. A separate post-freeze seam inventory records player-solvable contradictions;
   it never appears in the player-facing corpus.
9. Seven documents are enough only if each changes what later authors may treat
   as established fact.
