# SubLaborix Universal — Private Company Canon

**Status:** Internal generation authority. Not player-facing.

SubLaborix Universal sells guaranteed access to human labor as a fixed-price
enterprise subscription. Its customers do not hire named workers. They submit
tasks to a platform, and the platform routes each task to an available person
with the required skills.

The company calls participating workers **Autonomous Neural Nodes**, usually
shortened to **Nodes**. It maintains that a Node is an independent participant
who chooses when to enter the network, which tasks to accept, and how to perform
them. SubLaborix therefore says it is not an employer or staffing agency. It is
an orchestration platform that matches independent effort with enterprise
demand.

Its customer promise requires the opposite operational reality. Enterprise
clients buy guaranteed capacity, rapid assignment, and predictable completion
at a fixed monthly price. Delivering that promise requires SubLaborix to forecast
Node availability, reserve labor, rank and route workers, enforce response
windows, throttle low-performing Nodes, prevent simultaneous commitments, and
penalize refusal during demand periods.

## Founding thesis

Enterprise labor should be a pluggable service rather than headcount. Existing
gig platforms made individual jobs easier to post but did not let a company
reserve thousands of hours of reliable labor with the simplicity of cloud
capacity.

## Product doctrine

Employment is an inefficient bundle of separate effort units.

## Product

**Work as a Service (WaaS).** Enterprise subscribers submit tasks through an API
or operations console. The Cognitive Orchestration Engine decomposes each task,
matches it to qualified Nodes, monitors delivery, and reassigns work when needed.

## Economic primitive

- **What is sold:** reserved access to allocatable human effort.
- **Billable unit:** a fixed monthly subscription containing a stated pool of
  Node-hours and response guarantees.
- **Billing boundary:** the subscription fee is owed for reserved capacity,
  whether or not the client consumes it.
- **Primary cost boundary:** Node compensation and verification costs rise with
  actual consumption.
- **Recognized success:** enterprise renewal and service-level attainment, not
  worker stability or long-term task quality.

## Governing fiction

Actual activity: SubLaborix dynamically directs, measures, disciplines, and
reserves human labor so that enterprise customers receive predictable service.

Institutional classification: a decentralized Cognitive Mesh through which
independent Autonomous Neural Nodes make momentary, self-directed offers of
discrete effort.

## Central contradiction

SubLaborix must exercise enough control over workers to guarantee labor to its
customers, while avoiding enough control over workers to maintain that it does
not employ them.

No player-facing document should state this complete accusation. Each document
may accurately describe the part its author is responsible for.

## Secondary contradiction

The fixed subscription is most profitable when clients reserve more labor than
they use. The product is most valuable when clients consume all the labor they
were promised. Successful adoption therefore drives contribution margin down.

## Dependency architecture

- **Operational:** clients integrate recurring workflows and remove internal
  staffing capacity after the platform proves reliable.
- **Worker:** Nodes accumulate performance history, qualification levels, and
  platform credits that do not transfer elsewhere.
- **Legal:** the company depends on its Nodes remaining independent in name while
  its service levels depend on their predictable compliance.
- **Technical:** the performance ledger records assignment, acceptance, refusal,
  timing, output, rework, and throttling decisions.
- **Financial:** high-utilization clients become the least profitable accounts
  but the strongest sales references.
- **Institutional:** policy work seeks formal recognition of Cognitive Mesh
  Participation as something other than employment or contracting.

## Plain-language accusation

SubLaborix says its workers are free because treating them as employees would
destroy the business. It promises customers those same workers will always be
available because admitting they are free would destroy the product.

## Language rules

- Use **Node** in company records after defining it once.
- Use **worker** in private analysis, external diligence, and ordinary speech.
- Do not use quantum, multiversal, interdimensional, neural-upload, or other
  deliberately zany explanations.
- The company is alarming because intelligent people make defensible decisions
  inside incompatible incentives.
- Every operational term must be understandable from context by a high-school
  senior.
