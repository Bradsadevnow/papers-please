# Private Source Simulation — Customer Discovery Interviews

**Status:** Generation source. Not player-facing.

**Interview period:** January 25–February 19, 2027  
**Interviewers:** Lena Ortiz; Priya Nwosu  
**Purpose:** Test whether enterprises will reserve labor capacity and whether
workers will supply it under the proposed participation model.

## Camille Mercer — Director of Business Operations, Northstar Ledger Group

Northstar uses temporary staff during month-end reconciliation and acquisition
integration. Camille does not want another marketplace. Her team cannot manage
forty individual contractors while closing the books.

She would pay for a monthly capacity commitment if SubLaborix owns assignment,
replacement, quality review, and deadline recovery. She expects a qualified
replacement within thirty minutes when someone declines or disappears. She does
not care which person performs a task, provided access controls and work history
are preserved.

She would not pay $49,500 merely for introductions. Procurement will compare the
subscription with a managed-services agreement, not with freelance-platform
fees. Northstar may provide a pilot if it can prohibit named people from working
simultaneously for competitors during reserved coverage periods.

## Thomas Bell — Vice President of Claims Operations, Harbor Mutual

Harbor's demand is highly seasonal. Thomas likes predictable spend but expects
to consume nearly every purchased hour during catastrophe response. A plan built
around customer underuse would fail his procurement review.

He needs scheduled overnight and weekend coverage, not a pool that may be
available. He would accept slower assignment for ordinary work but wants a
contractual response time during declared events. He assumes SubLaborix, rather
than Harbor, is responsible for ensuring enough workers report when needed.

## Rachel Singh — Procurement Counsel, Westbridge Retail

Rachel would require one accountable vendor, audit rights, background checks,
data-processing terms, and evidence that workers cannot remove customer data.
She views detailed instructions, monitored output, mandatory security training,
and removal for poor performance as ordinary vendor controls.

She also warns that the more SubLaborix standardizes schedules, prices,
acceptance, and discipline, the less credible it becomes to say the people are
independent. A contract clause will not cure product behavior.

## Mateo Ruiz — Independent finance-operations contractor

Mateo wants work without repeated interviews and unpaid client acquisition. He
would declare twenty to thirty available hours per week if the platform produced
steady assignments.

He will not promise that every declared hour remains free unless standby is
paid. If he rejects a low-paying task and then stops receiving better work, he
will understand the rejection right as formal but not real. He values portable
proof of completed work and dislikes ratings that only have value inside one
platform.

## Alina Brooks — Customer-support contractor

Alina prefers choosing short assignments around family care. A thirty-minute
response requirement is workable only if she can revise availability without a
penalty. She asks who pays when a customer supplies incomplete instructions and
the task is reassigned as a quality failure.

She would accept platform credits for optional training, but not as wages or as
the only way to challenge a qualification decision.

## Jorge Mendes — Former staffing-branch manager

Jorge believes buyers will pay for SubLaborix only when it behaves like a
staffing operator while appearing technically easier to procure. The difficult
work is maintaining a ready bench, resolving no-shows, and absorbing demand
variance.

He expects the company to over-recruit workers, predict likely acceptance, and
reduce future offers to unreliable participants. In his experience, guaranteed
coverage requires either paid standby, scheduled shifts, or more supply than the
financial model initially wants to carry.

## Source tensions retained for synthesis

- Buyers distinguish managed capacity from introductions.
- Buyers assign SubLaborix responsibility for worker availability and recovery.
- Workers value flexibility but do not consider unpaid standby reliable supply.
- Procurement wants monitoring and discipline while warning that they indicate
  employment-like control.
- High-need buyers expect full utilization, while founding economics benefit
  from unused capacity.
