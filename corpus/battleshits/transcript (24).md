>

Missing a delivery window becomes a productivity event. A supply interruption becomes a liability cascade. You've transformed a wellness product into critical infrastructure for your customers' operations—and you've done this while operating on the assumption that supplemental oxygen in normal physiological ranges produces measurable cognitive gains, which it doesn't.
> 
> Second-order: Your corporate path assumes regulatory approval. But once you start making productivity claims tied to oxygen delivery, you're now making medical claims. FDA jurisdiction triggers immediately. You cannot position this as 'premium air' and simultaneously claim it eliminates fatigue—that's a therapeutic claim. The moment you make that claim in marketing, you've conceded that AeroElite is a drug, not a consumer good. Your path to IPO requires you to either drop the core value proposition or submit to pharmaceutical timelines and approval gates that will cost $500M+ and 7-10 years. Your $750M pre-money valuation assumes you avoid that path. But your pitch assumes you don't.

Verdict: You've built a business model where the technical mechanism (oxygen delivery above physiological saturation) is biologically inert, the regulatory cost (medical device/drug approval) inverts your timeline, and the customer dependency you've created makes supply-chain failures into liability events. You're not scaling oxygen. You're scaling risk.
