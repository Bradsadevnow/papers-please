### I. Introduction and Purpose

This Knowledge Article sets standard terms for all internal documents about a customer's emotional resolution status, since Clinical Care, Product Development, Finance, and Employer Services have recently used the word "closure" in different ways (see C&O-Q4-2023-Minutes-RevB).

This article does not try to fix why these different usages exist. Instead, it makes sure every department uses a clear, specific term for 'closure' when writing reports or documents. If you assume another department means something else by a status, you must add context tags explaining that assumption.

### II. Defined Terminology Standards

We define four main terms related to the customer journey. Each term belongs to one specific department and has a unique meaning, so you cannot swap these terms without adding qualifying language.

**1. Successful Emotional Closure (SEC)**
*   **Definition:** This is a declaration made by a user through the platform interface. It means the user stated, at that moment, they achieved personal emotional resolution regarding the tracked concern. This is an *administrative milestone declaration*.
*   **Owner Department:** Product Development.
*   **Plain Language Meaning:** "The user told us they felt better."

**2. Clinical Resolution Status (CRS)**
*   **Definition:** This is a determination made by a licensed clinician. It means the patient reached a stable state of emotional acceptance and detachment from the core issue. This shows therapeutic progress.
*   **Owner Department:** Clinical Services.
*   **Plain Language Meaning:** "The doctor thinks they are truly over it."

**3. Billing-Risk Event (BRE)**
*   **Definition:** This is a recorded status change that forces a review of recurring billing obligations. For finance, this marks when the expected steady income stream is threatened or changed.
*   **Owner Department:** Finance.
*   **Plain Language Meaning:** "The system needs to check if we should be sending an invoice."

**4. Return to Productivity Status (RTP)**
*   **Definition:** This status is assigned by the employer client. It means the employee can return to full work duties with minimal help from GriefForge services, as stated in the Enterprise Client Service Level Agreement.
*   **Owner Department:** Employer Services.
*   **Plain Language Meaning:** "The company says they are ready to work again."

### III. Usage Guidelines and Qualification Requirements

When using these terms together in one document, the author *must* include a qualifier. This qualifier must specify which department's definition is being used. If you fail to qualify the term, Compliance will flag the document for mandatory revision.

**Example of Incorrect Use (To Avoid):** "The customer achieved closure and stopped paying."
*(Problem: This mixes SEC, BRE, and CRS without explaining it.)*

**Example of Required Use (Correct Format):** "Following their declaration of **Successful Emotional Closure (SEC)**, the Billing-Risk Event **(BRE)** was noted on [Date]. This triggered a review for potential **Return to Productivity Status (RTP)** assessment by Employer Services."
*(Note: The structure must show how one department's status affects another's paperwork.)*

### IV. Worked Examples Using Established Cohort Figures

These examples show the correct way to document status changes using data from Q3 2023 records. Each example uses a different mix of defined terms.

**Example A: Conflict Documentation (Product vs. Finance)**
A report citing **CAS-Q3-23** must state: "Of the 892 users who achieved **Successful Emotional Closure (SEC)** by September 30, 2023 [Product Definition], a subsequent review of billing data showed this status correlated with a $14.5 million negative impact on projected QRR for that period [Finance Observation]."

**Example B: Clinical vs. Employer Documentation**
A memo about an enterprise account must state: "The employee’s **Clinical Resolution Status (CRS)**, determined by Dr. Hayes, was not yet enough to justify declaring a full **Return to Productivity Status (RTP)** under the current SLA terms."

**Example C: Full Cross-Departmental Documentation Example**
A summary document detailing one case must read: "Client X reported achieving **Successful Emotional Closure (SEC)** on August 15th. While this declaration triggered a potential **Billing-Risk Event (BRE)**, clinical assessment indicates the patient has not yet reached full **Clinical Resolution Status (CRS)**, therefore preventing an immediate reassessment for **Return to Productivity Status (RTP)**."
