# GriefForge Product Outcomes Benchmark Report PO-BENCH-11/23

**Date Issued:** November 30, 2023
**Prepared For:** Internal Strategy Review Committee
**Subject:** Performance Benchmarking for the Phased Resolution Pilot Cohort (Pilot Period: Nov 1 – Nov 15)
**Reference Documents:** PR-77B; K-901.

---

### I. Purpose of Report

This report compares two ways of measuring customer success. We compare the old way of measuring resolution against a new measurement used in the Phased Resolution model. The goal is to measure how well our structured pacing process works. This process requires users to move through set stages before they are fully acknowledged as resolved. This benchmarking helps us track how much people use the platform compared to goals we already know for a small, controlled group (the pilot).

### II. Background: Old Benchmark (Pre-Phased Model)

The old way of measuring success used the **Successful Emotional Closure (SEC)** status declaration. This was the main sign that service was finished. Under this old system, a customer was only counted as fully resolved and "successful" for reports if they *voluntarily* said they no longer needed active support services. This single moment in time decided when the measurement ended.

For comparison, we looked at a **historical group** of 600 customers. These people finished their resolution using this old standard during Q3 2023. Of these 600 people, **96** were recorded as having reached SEC status during that time.

### III. New Benchmark: Resolution Progress Score (RPS)

The Phased Resolution model requires tracking progress across three required stages before a final acknowledgement can be made. Because of this, the new main measurement is the **Resolution Progress Score (RPS)**. This score rewards users for showing documented movement through these set milestones, rather than just counting the final declaration.

The RPS uses a 100-point scale based on finishing each stage in order:
*   Finishing Stage 1 (Initial Insight): Gives 35 points.
*   Finishing Stage 2 (Active Pattern Recognition): Gives 45 points.
*   Finishing Stage 3 (Final Integration Pre-Status): Gives 20 points.

**Important Note:** Getting **Clinical Resolution Status (CRS)** is a separate determination made by clinicians. It does not count toward the RPS score. To get this score, the final stage must be finished within the tracking period.

### IV. Pilot Cohort Results: November 1 – November 15

The pilot group had exactly 600 enrolled customers. Tracking during the first two weeks showed these progress counts:

*   Customers finishing Stage 1 (Initial Insight): 240 people.
*   Customers finishing Stage 2 (Active Pattern Recognition): 150 people.
*   Customers finishing Stage 3 (Final Integration Pre-Status): 60 people.
*   Customers reaching Clinical Resolution Status (CRS) by the end of the period: 18 people.

**RPS Calculation for Pilot Cohort:**
The RPS is found by adding up points based on the *highest stage achieved* by each person, up to a maximum of 100 points. Since there were 600 people in the group, we calculate the average score earned:

$$\text{Total Score Earned} = (240 \times 35) + (150 \times 45) + (60 \times 20)$$
$$\text{Total Score Earned} = 8,400 + 6,750 + 1,200$$
$$\text{Total Score Earned} = 16,350 \text{ points}$$

$$\text{Average RPS per Customer} = \frac{\text{Total Score Earned}}{\text{Pilot Cohort Size}}$$
$$\text{Average RPS per Customer} = \frac{16,350}{600} = 27.25 \text{ points}$$

*(Note: The average RPS of 27.25 shows the documented progress across all stages achieved by the group.)*

### V. Comparative Analysis and Interpretation

We compare the performance numbers side-by-side to show the value added by the new method:

| Metric | Historical Benchmark (N=600) | Pilot Cohort (N=600) | Change/Observation |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Primary Measurement** | SEC Declarations | Average RPS Score | The focus shifted from one final declaration to tracking weighted progress. |
| **Observed Count** | 96 Customers (SEC) | N/A (Score is continuous) | The focus moves from counting endpoints to measuring process completion. |
| **Performance Indicator** | N/A | Average RPS: 27.25 Points | Measures structured engagement through defined stages. |

The new RPS gives a detailed look at how much users invested in the process, which we couldn't measure before. While the old benchmark showed 96 people declaring SEC, the pilot group shows an average progress score of 27.25 points per person who completed the initial tracking period. This suggests steady and predictable use of our structured pacing modules.

**Important Clarification:** The 18 customers reaching CRS show clinical success but are not included in the RPS calculation. This is because this score measures *platform movement*, not medical status.

### VI. Limitations of Benchmarking Data

This report has limits that must be understood when reading the results:
1. **Scope Boundary:** The RPS only tracks progress through Stages 1, 2, and 3. It does not count any emotional growth or change happening outside of these tracked platform modules.
2. **Measurement Divergence:** The old SEC metric was a declaration; the current CRS is a clinical determination. These two types of status measurements answer completely different questions about how ready a customer is.
3. **Process Dependency:** The RPS calculation assumes users move through stages in a straight line, which might not match how people actually feel over time.

### VII. JARDOS Comparability Note

JARDOS confirms that the numbers here come from separate data sets: the SEC count (historical) is a report of what people said on their own; the CRS count is logged by clinical staff; and the RPS comes from tracking module completions. The system treats these three status types as independent variables. This means you cannot do simple math to compare them and claim an overall improvement or decline in actual clinical closure rates.
