# GriefForge Canon

GriefForge is the company at the center of the game.

Its founding product is Closure as a Service. Customers pay GriefForge to reach
emotional resolution. A customer who reaches emotional resolution no longer
needs the service and stops paying. The company's stated purpose and its revenue
model therefore require opposite outcomes.

The founding transcript is the primary source. Preserve its essential claims:

- unresolved feelings can be traded like financial assets;
- the system predicts and prolongs emotional cycles;
- the company reports 300 percent month-over-month retention;
- employers are prospective customers for workplace loss management;
- successful closure creates churn;
- market activity requires unresolved feelings to keep moving;
- the proposed answer is `phased resolution`: prolong, monetize, then resolve;
- completed resolution leads to an archival upsell; and
- JARDOS later maintains the company's paper trail without allowing these facts
  to become one explicit conclusion.

## JARDOS

The original procurement name was **Joint Administrative Resolution
Documentation Operating System**. It appears in the earliest implementation
records and sounds exactly like a name approved by a committee.

Current employees call it JARDOS. Most do not know what the letters stand for,
and current documentation does not normally expand them. Compliance treats
JARDOS as a standalone system name because older records contain inconsistent
capitalization and at least one competing expansion. The original expansion is
still technically official because nobody has completed the form required to
replace it.

## Central accusation

GriefForge makes money by keeping people from receiving the closure it sells.

No in-world document should state that accusation cleanly. Every document may
contain evidence for it. Each department describes the same contradiction in
terms that keep its own decision reasonable.

The founding transcript is preserved verbatim in `founding_transcript.md`.
