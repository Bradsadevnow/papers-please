# GRIEFFORGE — LEGAL

**Privileged and Confidential**  
**Date:** December 1, 2023  
**From:** Olivia Klein, General Counsel  
**To:** Marketing; Product  
**Re:** “4.7 Times More Progress”

## Short answer

You may say **“4.7 times more measurable progress.”**

You may not say customers recovered faster, healed faster, reached closure more
often, or felt better.

These are not small wording differences.

## What the numbers show

The old report followed 600 customers. Ninety-six of them said they had reached
closure. That is 16 percent.

The new pilot also followed 600 customers. Of those customers:

- 240 finished Stage 1;
- 150 finished Stage 2; and
- 60 finished Stage 3.

That gives us 450 customers who finished at least one stage, or 75 percent.
Seventy-five divided by sixteen is 4.6875. Marketing may round this to 4.7.

Only 18 customers in the pilot received clinical closure status. That figure is
not part of the proposed claim.

## Legal position

The 96 customers in the old report reached an endpoint. The 450 customers in the
new report completed a step. An endpoint and a step are different things.

The proposed sentence is acceptable because it uses the new definition of
progress. It would not be acceptable with the old definition.

The number is accurate. The comparison is not useful. Those are separate review
questions.

## Approved copy

> **Phased Resolution delivers 4.7 times more measurable progress.**

The following note must appear directly below the claim. It may be smaller than
the headline. It may not require a separate click.

> “Measurable progress” means completing at least one GriefForge program stage.
> It does not mean recovery, healing, clinical improvement, or closure.

## Do not use

- “4.7 times more customers reached closure.”
- “Customers recover 4.7 times faster.”
- “Phased Resolution improves clinical outcomes by 470 percent.”
- “Most customers got better.”

Product asked whether the final line could be used if “better” appeared in
quotation marks. It cannot.
