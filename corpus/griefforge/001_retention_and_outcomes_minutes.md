### 3. Customer Outcome Report
Mr. Chen presented customer outcome data, referencing Cohort Analysis Summary Q3/2023 (Document ID: CAS-Q3-23). The report tracked 4,521 active subscribers who entered a 'Resolution Cycle' during the quarter.
Of this total, 892 subscribers reported achieving "successful emotional closure" between July 1 and September 30. These individuals generated zero transactions within 30 days after closure. Their documented cancellation rate in the next month was 65% (N=580), which is much higher than the average baseline of 18%.
Conversely, subscribers who remained actively engaged in monitored cycles—those who had not reached a defined 'Resolution State' by the end of tracking—maintained an average engagement rate of 72% over the last three months. Mr. Chen noted this link between unresolved status and continued platform use.

### 4. Retention Report
Mr. Thorne presented the Q3 Financial Reconciliation (Document ID: FIN-REC-Q3/23). The analysis showed that revenue was greatly affected by successful closures. Specifically, for the 892 subscribers who closed in Q3, their subscription revenue stopped entirely at the end of the reporting month. This caused a negative impact of $14.5 million on projected Quarterly Recurring Revenue (QRR).
Mr. Thorne pointed out that removing 'Closure Success' from our revenue model would increase reported QRR by 23% for this group alone. He noted this gap between achieved outcome and actual income needs immediate structural review.

### 5. Proposed Phased Resolution Plan
In response to the financial data, Ms. Reed introduced Proposal Document PR-77B: The Phased Resolution Model. This proposal argues that treating closure as a simple yes/no event (resolved/not resolved) misrepresents the necessary user journey for sustained value.
The Product team suggests changing the product flow to include mandatory 'Staging Intervals.' Instead of immediate full resolution, users would move through three readiness levels: Initial Insight (Stage 1), Active Pattern Recognition (Stage 2), and Final Integration Pre-Status (Stage 3). Full closure status would only be granted after successfully completing Stage 3 over a minimum 45-day period. This delays the final measurable outcome by about three weeks, allowing continued service use before billing changes happen.

### 6. Employer Plan
Mr. Chen next addressed the Enterprise Client Service Level Agreement (SLA) review. The current employer plan allows clients to require participation in GriefForge modules up to a certain point before declaring employee 'Return to Productivity' status. This agreement structure is key because it keeps employee use of our services active, providing steady revenue for corporate partners, even if the employee’s personal emotional progress suggests otherwise.

### 7. Clinical and Ethics Review
Dr. Hayes opened the floor for an ethical review on product pacing standards. She objected to suggesting that prolonged required engagement has therapeutic value. Dr. Hayes stated, "I object to any documentation implying that delaying a person's natural process equals 'supportive care.' The clinical record shows true resolution involves acceptance and detachment; requiring emotional maintenance for financial reasons misunderstands grief pathology."
Mr. Chen logged the objection under Ticket ID: ETHICS-409A, noting it needs review by Compliance regarding potential changes to service guarantees. No change in committee voting structure was adopted due to this submission.

### 8. JARDOS Terminology Note
Following Dr. Hayes's input, a supplemental document came from our internal knowledge system (JARDOS): Knowledge Article K-901. This article addresses the conflict between "Stated Product Outcome" and "Monetizable Engagement Metric." The note clarifies that 'successful closure,' when used financially, only refers to the *declaration* of an endpoint by the user, not their actual emotional state. Therefore, all documents must separate clinical status from administrative declaration for department-wide consistency.

### 9. Decision and Recorded Vote
After reviewing PR-77B and integrating K-901, the committee discussed formally adopting staged progression. Due to the revenue dependence detailed in Section 4, the proposal moved forward. The term 'delayed closure' was deemed insufficient for external legal review given current regulations on patient timelines. Ms. Klein suggested replacing this phrase with legally safer terminology describing structured pacing toward an outcome.
After discussion, the committee voted to adopt the staged progression model using the official designation: **Phased Resolution**.

**Vote:**
*   Finance: For Phased Resolution (ensuring continuity of QRR).
*   Product: For Phased Resolution (operationalizing the service flow).
*   General Counsel: For Phased Resolution (providing necessary legal terminology buffer).

The motion passed 3 to 0. **Phased Resolution** is formally adopted as GriefForge's official process for managing customer lifecycle progression through emotional support services.
