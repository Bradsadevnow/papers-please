# GriefForge External Campaign Claims Review MKT-CLAIMS-12/23

**Date Issued:** December 4, 2023
**Prepared For:** Marketing Department Leadership Team
**Subject:** Review and Approval of Comparative Performance Claims for Q4 Campaigns.
**Reference Documents:** PO-BENCH-11/23; K-901 (Terminology Standard); PR-77B.

---

### I. Proposed Headline Claim

**Headline Draft:** 4.7 TIMES MORE MEASURABLE PROGRESS WITH PHASED RESOLUTION.

**Goal:** To show that the structured pacing model is better than old reporting methods. This aims to get people to sign up for a higher commitment during Q1 2024.

### II. Evidence Table and Arithmetic Review

The claim uses a ratio (a comparison) between two types of numbers: how many people reached an end point historically versus how much tracking volume there is now. We must check carefully that the top number and bottom number measure the same kind of 'progress.'

| Component | Value Used | Definition Source | Calculation Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Historical Group Endpoints (Numerator Basis)** | 96 Customers | PO-BENCH-11/23, Section II. | The count of customers who said they reached Successful Emotional Closure (SEC) in the old comparison group (N=600). This is one single milestone declaration. |
| **Pilot Progress Volume (Denominator Basis)** | 450 Customers | PO-BENCH-11/23, Section V Analysis. | The total count of customers whose highest completed step was Stage 1 (240), Stage 2 (150), or Stage 3 (60). This shows how many people moved through at least one defined process step. |
| **Calculated Ratio** | $450 / 96 \approx 4.6875$ | Arithmetic Check | The ratio is rounded to 4.7 for the marketing copy to look cleaner. |

**Product Review Note:** Product Development agrees with using all stage-completion counts (450) as a stand-in for "measurable progress." This compares them against the historical SEC count (96). This shows more recorded activity across the stages of care.

### III. Departmental Comments and Concerns

**A. Clinical Services Review:**
Clinical services object to saying that 'progress' can be measured by counting platform interactions or completed stages. As noted in K-901, **Clinical Resolution Status (CRS)** is a decision based on a clinical check-up. This cannot be matched mathematically with the number of stages finished or declarations made. The claim must not suggest that high measurable progress means actual emotional recovery.

**B. Legal Department Review:**
Legal advises being careful about the word 'Progress.' While the documents show Stages 1, 2, and 3 are steps toward an outcome, the word 'progress' suggests a continuous improvement or healing over time. The claim must only stick to concepts of *measurable activity* within the service structure. It must avoid words like 'recovery,' 'healing,' or 'closure.'

**C. Finance Department Review:**
Finance notes that the calculation uses every customer who finished any tracked stage (450). If it used only customers who finished the final tracked stage (60), the ratio would be $60 / 96 \approx 0.6$. Therefore, including all three stages is needed to produce the desired multiplier while remaining defensible as a measure of recorded activity.

### IV. Prohibited Alternatives (Mandatory Exclusions)

The following words or phrases are strictly banned from any outside writing about this claim because they might conflict with rules or mislead people:
*   Recovery
*   Healing
*   Closure
*   Better feeling
*   Emotional improvement

### V. Required Footnote (Mandatory Inclusion)

This footnote must go with the final headline on all campaign materials: *Measurable Progress is defined by documented completion of at least one structured platform stage as tracked in the Phased Resolution model, not by clinical assessment.*

### VI. Final Approved Copy and Usage Guidelines

**Approved Headline:**
4.7 TIMES MORE MEASURABLE PROGRESS WITH PHASED RESOLUTION.

**Usage Rules:**
1.  The headline must always be shown with the mandatory footnote (Section V).
2.  All marketing materials must refer to the process as "structured pacing" or "Phased Resolution."
3.  When talking about results, use the combined Stage 1, Stage 2, and Stage 3 completion counts for this specific comparison.
