# GriefForge Q3 Financial Reconciliation (Document ID: FIN-REC-Q3/23)

**Date:** October 20, 2023
**Prepared By:** Finance Department
**Subject:** Quarterly Performance Review and Revenue Impact Analysis for Period Ending September 30, 2023

---

### I. Executive Summary

This document compares the expected income (projected revenue) with the actual sign-ups and activity of users for the third quarter (Q3) of Fiscal Year 2023. The main goal is to measure how changes in user status affected the money coming in. We specifically note the difference between what users *say* about their emotional state and the actual subscription income received. Overall platform performance is strong. However, we saw a big change related to subscribers who reported achieving self-declared closure during this time.

### II. Key Metrics Overview

The following numbers are totals for the quarter:

*   **Active Subscribers (Total):** 4,521 accounts kept an active subscription and used the platform at least once in Q3.
*   **Successful Emotional Closure Declarations:** 892 subscribers formally told GriefForge staff that their main emotional problem was solved as of September 30, 2023. This declaration is a simple record made by the user through the website portal.
*   **Cancellations Following Closure:** Of the 892 people who reached Successful Emotional Closure, 580 accounts (which is a **65%** rate) had no billing activity in the next month we reviewed.
*   **Baseline Cancellation Rate:** The usual average cancellation rate for all types of subscribers stays at **18%**.

### III. Revenue Analysis: Quarterly Recurring Revenue (QRR) Impact

Quarterly Recurring Revenue, or QRR, is the predictable money expected to come in regularly over three months from ongoing service subscriptions. This report looks at how certain user status changes affect this prediction.

When subscribers who declared Successful Emotional Closure stopped paying bills, it caused a big negative effect on predicted QRR for the quarter. The impact linked to these 892 accounts was **$14.5 million** against projected revenue. This is the difference between the income Finance expected and the income it now expects to receive.

On the other hand, looking at the group that did *not* declare Successful Emotional Closure showed they kept using the service. These users whose issues were not resolved stayed engaged by an average of **72%** over the three months. This supports continuing to predict QRR.

### V. Forecast Assumptions and Adjustments

The following assumptions are used to predict the QRR for Q4:

1.  **Closure Success Impact:** The money model treats Successful Emotional Closure as a negative income event. This means the declaration forces a stop on billing until support staff manually restart payments.
2.  **Baseline Use:** The 18% baseline cancellation rate remains the main number used to track users who leave for reasons other than closure success.
3.  **Cohort Value:** For predicting money, taking out the financial impact from the 'Closure Success' group results in a **23% reported increase** in predicted QRR for the quarter.

### VI. Identified Risks

1.  **Declaration Dependency Risk:** Because we rely on users to declare their status themselves, the system cannot track emotional changes that happen outside of using the platform. This could mean we underreport how much service continuity is actually needed.
2.  **Attrition Rate Skew:** The 65% cancellation rate after closure is a big jump compared to the 18% baseline. We must watch this closely to make sure it doesn't mess up future money predictions.
3.  **Sustainability Gap Risk:** There is a consistent gap between when users report reaching emotional goals (Successful Emotional Closure) and when they keep paying for the subscription. This is a structural financial risk if we don't fix it.
