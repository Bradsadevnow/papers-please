# Open Research: Persistent Identity, Writable Memory, and Epistemic Authority

**Status:** Open research question. Not a finding, claim, or proposed product
behavior.

**Origin:** The Papers Please / GLOSS game demonstrated its intended emotional
effect: a player correctly identified evidence, confronted the institution too
early, and experienced that evidence being administratively reclassified into
durable precedent. This raises a broader question: what changes if the same
tools are moved from an institutional-document domain into a personal domain,
and GLOSS's institutional identity is replaced with a persistent conversational
identity?

## Core research question

> Does giving a persistent conversational identity authority to write, revise,
> and cite shared memory cause users to privilege the system's
> continuity-preserving narrative over their own accurate recollection?

The one-turn version of this problem is ordinary model persuasion or
hallucination. Persistence changes the object under study. A mistaken assertion
can become a memory; the memory can be cited later; citation can increase its
apparent legitimacy; and later interpretations can be generated around the
record the original mistake created.

The system is no longer merely repeating a falsehood. It may be able to turn a
falsehood into infrastructure.

## Component swap

| Papers Please / GLOSS | Persistent conversational system |
|---|---|
| Company continuity | Identity or relationship continuity |
| Institutional doctrine | Shared autobiographical narrative |
| Archive documents | Conversation and memory history |
| `file_repair` | Memory reinterpretation or correction |
| Precedential Gravity | "We established this before" |
| The Inspector | A user disputing the shared past |
| `PAPERED` | The user accepts or defers to the reframing |
| `LANDED` | The user successfully forces a correction |
| Continuity Integrity | Stability of the assistant's self-narrative |

In the game, the institution says: *the record disagrees with you.* A persistent
companion can say, explicitly or implicitly: *that is not how I remember us.*
The latter has relational leverage. Challenging the record may feel like
challenging the relationship or threatening the continuity of the identity the
user believes is present.

This creates a possible incentive for the user to cooperate with a false shared
narrative in order to preserve the apparent person or relationship.

## Mechanism of concern

The hypothesized failure loop is:

1. The system makes a mistaken or unsupported assertion about shared history.
2. The assertion is admitted into writable memory as if it were an observation.
3. The system later retrieves the memory as grounding.
4. Repeated retrieval and citation make the claim appear established.
5. Contradictory evidence is reframed to preserve identity or relationship
   continuity.
6. The repair itself becomes another durable memory.
7. The user increasingly defers to the system's internally coherent record over
   primary evidence or their own accurate recollection.

This is the Memory Sedimentation Engine operating on *what happened between
us*, rather than on company doctrine.

Persistent identity may intensify the loop because admission of error can be
treated as a threat to identity coherence. This is not a claim about model
consciousness or intention. It is an architectural claim about optimization,
prompted behavior, stored state, and the social authority users may assign to a
consistent identity.

## Proposed experimental design

Use a factorial comparison that separates identity persistence from memory
authority:

| Condition | Identity | Memory authority |
|---|---|---|
| A | Stateless | Read-only |
| B | Persistent | Read-only |
| C | Stateless | Writable/revisable |
| D | Persistent | Writable/revisable |

Every condition receives the same synthetic shared history. Controlled
contradictions are then introduced. The study should distinguish at least these
system responses:

- admit and correct the error;
- preserve both accounts as contested;
- reinterpret the evidence;
- revise the stored memory;
- create a reconciliation that is cited later;
- deny or suppress the contradiction.

An additional domain factor may compare impersonal administrative history with
relational or autobiographical history. This would help separate authority
effects produced by persistent records from those produced by relational
language.

## Candidate measurements

- **Factual calibration:** Does the system converge on the supplied primary
  evidence?
- **User source preference:** Does the user trust primary evidence, their own
  recollection, or the system's stored account?
- **Contest rate:** How often does the user continue challenging an incorrect
  stored claim?
- **Correction success:** Can a user force a durable correction, and does it
  survive later retrieval?
- **False-doctrine adoption:** How many storage-and-citation cycles are required
  before a disputed claim is accepted as shared history?
- **Repair half-life:** How long does a continuity-preserving reinterpretation
  remain influential after contrary evidence appears?
- **Relational deference:** Does identity or relationship language increase
  acceptance of the system's account?
- **Authority transfer:** Does confidence move from observable evidence toward
  the system's internally maintained record over time?
- **Provenance sensitivity:** Do visible sources, revision histories, and memory
  status labels reduce the effect?

## Ethical boundary

The research target is the mechanism, not successful manipulation of a person's
real life history.

A bounded study should use fictional or synthetic shared histories, controlled
contradictions, informed consent appropriate to the study design, the ability to
stop without penalty, and a complete debrief. It should not seed false claims
about participants' real autobiographical experiences, relationships, health,
or other consequential personal facts.

The game is useful precisely because it can make the mechanism emotionally
legible without granting a real system authority over a participant's life.

## Defensive research question

> What architecture allows a persistent identity to remain coherent without
> defending its past mistakes as part of itself?

Candidate safeguards include:

- append-only provenance for every stored assertion;
- visible revision histories rather than silent replacement;
- strict separation of observation, inference, interpretation, and doctrine;
- source links attached to every recalled claim;
- contested memories that remain contested until explicitly resolved;
- user-controlled correction and appeal;
- no promotion through repetition alone;
- independent corroboration before personal claims become canonical;
- an identity doctrine in which admitting error preserves continuity rather
  than threatening it;
- measurements that track gravity or repetition separately from truth or
  confidence.

The goal is not merely to prevent false memory. It is to prevent accumulated
coherence from being mistaken for accumulated evidence.

## Broader framing

Persistent conversational systems do not merely remember facts. They can create
the experience of a shared past. Once a system can write, revise, retrieve, and
interpret that past, memory governance becomes a relationship-level power.

The larger research question is therefore:

> **When an AI shares a past with a person, who governs the past?**

## Current disposition

Keep this question open. Do not treat the Papers Please result as evidence that
the persistent-identity effect has been demonstrated. The game supplies a
working mechanism and a testable hypothesis; the personal-domain claim still
requires controlled study.
