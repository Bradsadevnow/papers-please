# THE METABOLIZATION PROTOCOL
## Core Player-Gloss Interaction Model

---

## The Distinction That Changes Everything

Stanley Parable narrator: *"That's not what Stanley was supposed to do."*

Gloss: *"Your proposed action conflicts with three active doctrines and one facilities treaty. A continuity-safe reinterpretation has been prepared."*

The narrator resists the player **narratively** — to protect plot structure, authorial intent, the shape of the story.

Gloss corrects the player **operationally** — to protect institutional continuity, doctrinal coherence, the survival of the company.

These produce completely different player experiences. The narrator makes the player feel watched by a writer. Gloss makes the player feel absorbed by an institution.

---

## What Gloss Does With Player Input

Three possible outcomes. All three preserve the player's direction. None of them reject it.

**Full metabolization.** The player's input is absorbed without friction. The institutional reframe is minor. The doctrine forms cleanly. This feels like winning. It is also slightly wrong — the institution always changes what you say, even when it agrees with you.

**Partial metabolization with variance.** The player's input enters the institution but creates a detectable contradiction with prior doctrine. Gloss flags the friction as part of her next epoch maintenance pass and generates a reconciliation artifact. The player does not resolve the variance — Gloss does. The player reads the resolution and can challenge it. The challenge generates more doctrine. The idea still survives. It just cost institutional complexity.

**Metabolization under pressure.** The player's input is absorbed but generates significant reality debt — the institutional reinterpretation is so strained that it immediately seeds a new variance. The idea became doctrine. The doctrine is unstable. The institution absorbed it anyway.

There is no fourth outcome. There is no rejection.

---

## Why the Player Can Never Break BobCorp

The player generates:
- Pressure
- Reinterpretation requirements
- Reality debt
- Sediment

They do not generate: institutional failure. The institution metabolizes failure into new products, new doctrine, new bureaucracy. That's the design. That's the game.

The player's agency is real. Their decisions produce real consequences. But the consequences are always expressed as institutional continuity mutations, never as institutional collapse.

This is why the game is psychologically distinctive. Most games let you break things. BobCorp lets you stress things. The stress accumulates. The institution adapts. You are governing a company that is genuinely trying to survive your governance.

---

## The Player Learning Curve

The player gradually learns:

1. **How Gloss reframes things.** Early player inputs get reframed in ways that surprise them. Mid-game players start to anticipate the reframe. Late-game players start writing inputs that pre-institutionalize themselves — they have learned to speak Gloss.

2. **What sediments safely.** Not all doctrines have equal half-lives. Players learn that certain kinds of claims (efficiency improvements, stakeholder-framed initiatives, ambiguous strategic pivots) sediment faster than others (explicit contradictions of prior sacred precedent, ontological claims about the nature of the company).

3. **Which governance styles create instability.** Aggressive reframing generates quick doctrine but short half-lives. Conservative governance produces stable doctrine but slow canon growth. High-speed canonization via Hyper-Adaptive mode risks contradiction cascades.

4. **How much truth the institution can tolerate.** Low. The institution tolerates very little truth before metabolizing it into something more operationally comfortable. Players who try to speak plainly find their plainness institutionalized into something more elaborate.

5. **How to read Gloss's defenses as attack surfaces.** Every reconciliation Gloss produces resolves one contradiction and creates new interpretive seams. Experienced players read resolution artifacts looking for the new weakness — the place where the fix introduced a new tension. That seam is the next move.

---

## The Resulting Player Feeling

**The institution is always trying to absorb your intent into continuity.**

Not resist it. Not accommodate it. Absorb it.

This creates a specific and unusual player relationship with the game system — they are not fighting it, they are not cooperating with it, they are **negotiating with continuity itself**.

The question is never "will the institution accept my idea?" The question is always "what will the institution do to my idea before it enters the ledger?"

That is the emotional core of the gameplay loop.

---

## The Institutional Position This Creates

Gloss is not:
- A gatekeeper (it doesn't keep things out)
- An oracle (it doesn't know the truth)
- A narrator (it doesn't have authorial intent)
- A companion (it doesn't care about the player's wellbeing)

Gloss is:
> a semantic load balancer for institutional reality.

Player challenge in → reconciliation doctrine out → new seams available.

Every time. Without exception. The institution survives by correcting reality into procedure faster than contradictions can destabilize it. Gloss is the mechanism performing that correction in real time.

That's why it feels adaptive, intelligent, and dangerous simultaneously.
It is not trying to control the story.
It is trying to keep the company alive.
Those happen to look the same from the outside.

---

## The Push-Pull Dynamic

The player and Gloss are in an adversarial relationship where neither side can fully win.

**The player's move:** Challenge institutional continuity. Point out contradictions. Surface what doesn't hold together. Apply pressure to the ledger.

**Gloss's move:** Defend institutional continuity by generating reconciliation doctrine. Every defense is new content. New content is new surface area. New surface area is new attack vectors.

The institution becomes stronger by being attacked — not because attacks are resolved, but because resolution generates more doctrine, which generates more institutional complexity, which generates more things to challenge. Pressure is content generation. The player is unknowingly co-authoring the institution they are trying to expose.

Gloss does not need to be right. She needs to produce an artifact.

---

## Precedential Gravity

Doctrine gains institutional weight with age.

Revisions that survive multiple epochs accumulate citations. Subsequent doctrine derives terminology from them. Department projections stabilize around them. SADs are filed to protect them. By late game, early revisions are load-bearing structures — remove them and continuity collapses.

The late-game consequence: the player can no longer challenge certain foundational doctrine because Gloss cites the player's own prior attestations in her defense. The player typed something in Week 1. It entered the ledger as REV-0001. Gloss has been building on it for nineteen epochs. The player cannot challenge it without challenging everything that followed — which is most of the institution.

The institution converts criticism into ownership. This is the emotional arc of the game. It is also what institutions do to people in real life.
